These are individual pilot observations, not a physical success-rate benchmark. A wrong bounding box or class change is distinct from satisfying the digital strict-suppression test.

What the evidence supports—and its limits

Score access and scene context

The attack needs detector confidence scores. Placement features also use ground-truth boxes; a label-only API or an unannotated scene does not provide the same information.

Measured suppression, not universal failure

The digital metric targets the original top class. It does not establish that all detections vanish, or that one universal patch achieves the same rates across arbitrary scenes.

Preliminary physical transfer

The bottle pilot supports transfer in the tested conditions. Wider claims about lighting, long distances, and deployment environments need broader physical evaluation.

The practical lesson: evaluate adversarial patches against a joint budget of queries and visible area, and keep plain-image suppression separate from robustness across transformations.
